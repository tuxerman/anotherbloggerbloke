# Blog Post Quality Review - January 2025

**Context**: Author is 35 now. Posts span 2013-2020, so ages ~22-29.

---

## Posts Reviewed

### 1. "Gentlemen's hanky guide to etiquette" (2013) - Age ~22

**The Good:**
- Genuinely funny premise and execution
- The voice is distinctive and playful
- Nice observational humor ("Hash you, Wah Bishop, Hashish")
- The Jason Bateman gif reference lands well

**Areas to Consider:**
- The gendered framing ("fellas", "gentlemen") and the "shoulder real-estate to worried and troubled females" line reads dated in 2025. The humor doesn't require this framing - the comedy about sneeze etiquette works universally.
- Some sentences are *very* long and convoluted (the first paragraph is essentially one mega-sentence). Breaking these up would improve readability.
- The parenthetical asides are charming but occasionally overdone.

**Suggestion:** A light edit to remove the gendered bits would preserve all the humor while making it more timeless. The "(Coonoor is near Ooty, which is sufficient grounds...)" aside is delightful and very *you* - keep that energy.

---

### 2. "Pen Hospital" (2014) - Age ~23

**The Good:**
- This is excellent writing. Genuinely moving.
- Beautiful sensory details ("like a stick of butter over a warm tawa")
- The structure works well - childhood, the fall, the hospital, present-day reflection
- The broader meditation on analog vs digital, craft vs convenience, is handled with subtlety
- "The fall of the machine from being a living breathing individual... to being a mere tool" - lovely phrasing
- The ending is perfect - doesn't over-explain

**Areas to Consider:**
- The "(Goodness, Sriram! Cahtuh, not Kaartar)" - wonderful, keep
- "Ammachiyane pareekshakku varum!" - readers without Malayalam may be lost. Consider a subtle gloss (though part of me says: let it be, it adds texture)
- Minor: "brethen" should be "brethren"

**Verdict:** This is publishable in any literary magazine. One of the strongest pieces. Don't touch it much.

### 3. "Stalin-gradschool" (2013) - Age ~22

**The Good:**
- Tight, economical storytelling
- The doctor is rendered vividly in few strokes ("he was probably the leg shaker type")
- Nice historical tidbit about Bulganin
- The punchline lands

**Areas to Consider:**
- The final line ("how communism was just a load of bull") - is this the doctor's view you're reporting, or your own? In 2013 it reads as the doctor's amusing non-sequitur. In 2025, some readers might read it differently. Clarifying attribution could help (or just leave it - it's clearly the doctor speaking).

**Verdict:** Short, charming vignette. Works as-is.

---

### 4. "Agreeing on how to disagree" (2016) - Age ~25

**The Good:**
- Important idea, especially relevant in 2025
- The country/individual analogy is thoughtful
- Quotes Shashi Tharoor - fits the theme

**Areas to Consider:**
- This reads more like a LinkedIn post or a thread than the vivid personal essays elsewhere
- It's quite abstract - no concrete stories or examples to anchor the ideas
- "aashirvadam" is a nice touch but the piece could use more of that texture

**Suggestion:** This is the weakest of the sample because it lacks the specificity and narrative that make your other pieces sing. If keeping it, consider adding a concrete anecdote that illustrates the principle. Or: accept it as a different mode (short-form opinion) and keep it brief.

### 5. "Despatches from the throne" (2017) - Age ~26

**The Good:**
- Hilarious. The commitment to the euphemism throughout is masterful.
- "M. Shoes" and the Shakespearean "exeunt stage right" - chef's kiss
- "fluid verbosity" - you know what you did there
- The three-stage emotional progression (disgust → humor → sympathy) is nicely observed
- "the serpent's egg" Brutus reference is wonderful

**Areas to Consider:**
- The opening paragraph's search for quiet space could be tightened slightly
- "enthu-cutlet" is perfect Indian-English but may confuse some readers (though I'd keep it)

**Verdict:** Don't change this. It's a comedy gem. The elaborate Victorian circumlocution about bathroom noises is exactly right.

### 6. "Old souls" (2017) - Age ~26

**The Good:**
- Vulnerable, earnest writing about a difficult-to-articulate feeling
- The temple observation (Arsikere and Vienna as equivalent) is beautiful
- "I just realise I've utterly failed at recreating this feeling with words" - honest and endearing
- Links are integrated well (Sicut Cervus, The Egg)
- "It has been a rough year" - the sudden personal note grounds everything

**Areas to Consider:**
- Some sentences run long and could be broken up
- "for lack of a better phrase, muse about" - consider cutting the hedge
- The piece is genuine but walks close to the "profound late-night thoughts" genre that can age poorly. Yours is saved by the self-awareness and concrete details.

**Verdict:** Keep. The sincerity is disarming and the feeling is real.

### 7. "Mirabel" (2019) - Age ~28

**The Good:**
- Atmospheric, almost magical realist
- "The signboard is ornamental" - yes
- "sun-with-a-face, lurking in a dark garden" - evocative
- The dreamlike quality is sustained throughout
- Short, controlled, knows when to end
- "What, then, is dusk?" - perfect ending

**Areas to Consider:**
- Very minor: "How wonderful would it be if this path didn't exist otherwise" - slightly awkward phrasing. Maybe "How wonderful if this path didn't exist otherwise" or "What if this path only existed now"

**Verdict:** Beautiful piece. This is mature writing. The restraint compared to earlier pieces shows growth.

## Overall Observations

### Strengths Across the Collection
1. **Distinctive voice** - Erudite but warm, playful but sincere
2. **Sensory detail** - You notice things and render them well
3. **Humor** - Ranges from wordplay to observational to absurdist; always controlled
4. **Cultural texture** - Malayalam, Hindi, references to India sit naturally
5. **Growth visible** - 2019-2020 pieces are more controlled, less try-hard than 2013

### Patterns to Watch
1. **Long sentences** - Your early work especially has paragraphs that are single sentences. Modern readers prefer shorter units.
2. **Gendered assumptions** - A few early pieces assume male reader or have dated gender dynamics
3. **Hedging** - "for lack of a better phrase", "if you will" - sometimes these work, sometimes they undermine confidence
4. **Abstract pieces** - The essay-style posts (Agreeing to Disagree) are weaker than narrative pieces

### Recommendations for 2025 Publication

1. **Pen Hospital** - Flagship piece. Light copyedit only.
2. **Despatches from the throne** - Comedy highlight. Keep as-is.
3. **Mirabel** - Showcases mature style. Tiny phrasing tweak.
4. **Hanky guide** - Remove gendered language; the humor stands alone.
5. **Stalin-gradschool** - Works as-is, nice quick read.
6. **Old souls** - Keep for its sincerity; minor tightening.
7. **Agreeing to Disagree** - Either cut or accept as a different format.
8. **Treetops** - Photo post, fine as-is.

### Typos/Errors Found
- "brethen" → "brethren" (Pen Hospital)

---

## Final Note

You were a good writer at 22 and became a better one by 28. The 2014 Pen Hospital piece could be published anywhere tomorrow. The growth from "look at my clever sentences" (2013) to "let the moment speak" (2019 Mirabel) is clear.

The question for 2025: What do you want to write *now*? The observational humor? The atmospheric vignettes? The meditative pieces? All are viable, but Mirabel and Pen Hospital suggest where your voice is strongest.
